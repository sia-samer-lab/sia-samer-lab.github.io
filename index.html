About us

Robotics for challenging space environments

Founded in April 2025

The Space Autonomous Manipulation and Exploration Robotics (SAMER) Lab develops robotic systems for space applications. Our research focuses on autonomous manipulation, mobility, and exploration technologies that enable robots to operate effectively in challenging space environments. By integrating robotics, artificial intelligence, and space engineering, we aim to develop reliable and adaptable systems for in-orbit servicing, planetary exploration (Lunar, Mars, and Asteriod Mission), and future space operations.

We bring together perception, planning, control, and intelligent decision-making to help robots operate with greater autonomy and reliability in orbit and on planetary surfaces.

The meaning behind SAMER Read the story

Different people, one community.

Beyond its role as an acronym, SAMER also carries a cultural meaning. The name playfully echoes “same” and the Chinese ideal of datong (大同)—a community built on harmony, mutual support, and shared progress. At SAMER Lab, people from different backgrounds learn from one another, help one another, and grow together. Everyone who joins becomes part of the same team and the same family.

What we study

Research areas

Our work connects robotics, autonomy, intelligent perception, and space engineering.

Autonomous manipulation

Algorithms and robotic systems for assembly, maintenance, and sample handling with limited human intervention.

Planetary exploration

Robust robotic platforms for autonomous navigation and scientific investigation on planetary surfaces.

AI for space robotics

Machine learning, perception, and decision-making methods for uncertain and unstructured environments.

Orbital servicing

Robotic technologies for satellite servicing, repair, refueling, and responsible debris removal.

Extreme-environment robotics

Mechanisms and control strategies designed for vacuum, radiation, temperature extremes, and difficult terrain.

Multi-robot systems

Coordination and collaboration strategies for teams of robots carrying out complex space missions.

Prof. Wei Zhang is a Director and Principal Investigator at the Shenyang Institute of Automation, CAS. He has led key technologies for the China Space Station glovebox and the Chang'e-5 lunar panoramic camera turntable, alongside research in space automation.
